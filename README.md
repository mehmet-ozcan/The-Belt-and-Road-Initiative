# 📊 The Belt and Road Initiative: A Spatial Empirical Analysis of Economic Performance in Central Asia and the South Caucasus  

This repository accompanies the article:  

**Kahraman, A., & Özcan, M. (2024). The Belt and Road Initiative: A spatial empirical analysis of economic performance in Central Asia and the South Caucasus. _Journal of the Asia Pacific Economy._ https://doi.org/10.1080/13547860.2024.2325252**

---

## 📘 About the Study  

This research investigates the economic implications of China’s **Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)** on nine economies along the China–Central Asia–West Asia corridor:  
**Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Azerbaijan, Armenia, Georgia, and Iran.**  

Using **panel-spatial econometric models**, the study evaluates:  
- Whether the BRI announcement in 2013 significantly influenced per capita income.  
- The role of **Chinese foreign direct investment (FDI)** and **mutual trade** in shaping economic growth.  
- The existence of **spatial spillover effects** among neighboring economies.  

**Key finding:** While **FDI inflows from China** did not exert significant growth effects, **trade with China** emerged as the dominant channel through which the BRI positively impacted regional economies.  

---

## 🛠 Repository Contents  

This repository provides all data-handling and estimation codes used in the study, written in **STATA**.  

---

## 📂 Data Sources  

The dataset combines macroeconomic and trade indicators covering **2004–2020**:  

- **UNCTADstat** → GDP per capita, capital formation, and population.  
- **United Nations Comtrade** → Exports and imports to/from China.  
- **SBCOFDI (National Bureau of Statistics of China)** → Chinese FDI inflows.  
- **Authors’ own construction** → Belt & Road dummy variable (pre-2013 = 0; post-2013 = 1).
